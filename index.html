Federated Learning with Homomorphic Encryption: An Analysis of Techniques for Secure Aggregation and Efficient Communication (2023)

Masters Thesis, Universität Hamburg

This project focuses on the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in healthcare to improve medical decision-making and patient outcomes. AI has the ability to analyze and process large amounts of complex medical data in real-time and provide more accurate predictions and diagnoses. However, there are several challenges that need to be addressed to fully leverage the benefits of AI in healthcare, such as data quantity, data privacy and security, data quality, and regulatory and ethical considerations. Federated learning (FL) is a cutting-edge AI technique that enables the creation of machine learning models from decentralized data sources, stored on individual devices or edge nodes. However, FL faces challenges in the healthcare industry due to non-iid data and privacy regulations for medical data.

To address these challenges, FedProx is an FL framework that allows for different devices to perform a variable amount of work based on their available resources. Techniques like secure multiparty computation (SMPC), differential privacy, and homomorphic encryption (HE) can be used to handle privacy concerns in healthcare. HE is particularly effective as it enables computation on encrypted data without the need for decryption, thus keeping the data private throughout the process.

The objective of this project is to evaluate the feasibility of using FL for cancer prediction in a healthcare setting, starting with a simple neural network and progressing to more complex neural networks, such as multi-modal networks.

IndoRE: Relation Extraction for Low Resource Indian Languages (2022)

Kaggle Competition and NLP Course Project, Universität Hamburg

The IndoRE project focused on relation extraction for three low-resource Indian languages: Bengali, Telugu, and Hindi. With relation extraction being a crucial task in natural language processing (NLP), the objective was to predict the relationship between a pair of entities given a sentence.

Due to the scarcity of tagged data for relation classification in Indian languages, the project aimed to explore and enhance this area by leveraging recent advancements in deep generative models. The challenge involved predicting relations from limited tagged training data, consisting of instances across 25 different relations for each language. Additionally, an untagged data dump was provided, which facilitated the use of techniques like similarity-based data augmentation or distant supervision to improve performance.

Employing mBERT and RoBERTa models, the project successfully tackled the relation extraction task. By leveraging these powerful models and exploring innovative techniques, the project pushed the boundaries of relation classification in low-resource Indian languages.

Overall, the IndoRE project served as an opportunity to showcase expertise in NLP, particularly in relation extraction for Bengali, Telugu, and Hindi. It demonstrated the ability to leverage limited tagged data and explore data augmentation and distant supervision techniques to improve model performance in the context of low-resource languages.

How Hateful are Movies? A study and prediction on Movie Subtitles (2021)

KONVENS Conference

In this research, we investigate techniques to detect hate speech in movies. We introduce a new dataset collected from the subtitles of six movies, where each utterance is annotated either as hate, offensive or normal. We apply transfer learning techniques of domain adaptation and fine-tuning on existing social media datasets, namely from Twitter and Fox News. We evaluate different representations, i.e., Bag of Words (BoW), Bi-directional Long short-term memory (Bi-LSTM), and Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) on 11k movie subtitles. The BERT model obtained the best macro-averaged F1-score of 77%. Hence, we show that transfer learning from the social media domain is efficacious in classifying hate and offensive speech in movies through subtitles.

Model-Based Reinforcement Learning: MuZero (2021)

Practical Seminar, Knowledge Processing in Intelligent Systems, Universität Hamburg

The project focuses on the application of model-based reinforcement learning (RL) using the MuZero algorithm. Model-free RL has shown great success in recent years, but it lacks direct applicability to real-world domains like robotics or intelligent systems. Model-based RL overcomes this limitation by first learning the environment dynamics and then planning and predicting based on that learned model.

MuZero builds upon the model-based RL approach and incorporates AlphaZero's search and search-based policy iteration. The algorithm recurrently considers current observations, forms hidden states, and uses previous observations to predict actions, value functions, and immediate rewards. By combining planning and learning, MuZero enables effective decision-making in complex environments.

The project also utilizes Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to explore and optimize decision trees. MCTS selects nodes with the highest winning probability, expands nodes to consider future moves, and uses reinforcement learning to guide exploration. Through Monte Carlo Simulation, it identifies high-reward trajectories in the search tree, offering an efficient alternative to brute-force methods.

The project report provides a comprehensive overview of the MuZero algorithm's development, from its predecessors like AlphaGo to its current state. It describes the algorithm in detail, explores visualizations of the model's latent space, and presents experimental results for both grid-based and non-grid-based games. The findings and conclusions from these experiments are discussed, highlighting the potential of MuZero in various domains.

Detecting Correct/Incorrect/No Mask through images using YoloV3 (2021)

Project, Computer Vision 2, Universität Hamburg

The project focuses on real-time face mask detection using the YOLOv3 algorithm, leveraging the Properly Wearing Masked Face Detection Dataset (PWMFD). The system is designed to accurately identify individuals wearing face masks in real-time.

To adapt the original script (Darknet) for our specific requirements, several modifications were implemented. Firstly, we developed a utility Python file to convert annotation files in XML format to the required YOLO format (TXT files with normalization). Additionally, changes were made to the YOLO configuration file, adjusting the classes and filter parameters. The filter size was calculated based on the number of anchors and classes, ensuring optimal performance (in our case, 3 * (5 + 3) = 24).

Due to GPU memory constraints, the number of batches, epochs, and subdivisions were adjusted. To execute the model successfully, we downloaded pre-trained convolutional layer embeddings and cloned the darknet repository. Modifications were made to the Makefile to enable GPU, CUDA, and OPENCV utilization. To adhere to YOLO's execution constraints, a specific folder structure for data was implemented. This included organizing the images and labels in separate folders under the data directory, along with the creation of train.txt and val.txt files listing the training and validation files, respectively.

Finally, we developed a script to collect the final outputs from the detection set and generate visualizations for error analysis. This allows for a comprehensive evaluation of the model's performance and identification of areas for improvement. Overall, our project provides a reliable and efficient solution for real-time face mask detection, contributing to the global efforts in promoting public health and safety.

Training a Small-Scale BERT Model on an NLP Question Answering Task (2021)

Project, Bio-Inspired Artificial Intelligence, Universität Hamburg

In this project, we explore the use of mobileBERT, a small-scale transformer-based language model, for training NLP question answering tasks using the bAbI dataset provided by Facebook. Our objective is to compare the performance of mobileBERT with the widely used SQuAD dataset, which was utilized during the introduction of BERT and mobileBERT.

To achieve this, we conducted preprocessing on the dataset and customized the model parameters to maximize accuracy for three specific bAbI tasks: "Single Supporting Fact," "Two Argument Relations," and "Conjunction." We also tested BERT, a state-of-the-art language model for NLP, on these tasks and compared the results with mobileBERT.

Our findings indicate that mobileBERT can be effectively adapted to solve these tasks, demonstrating promising accuracy rates. Particularly, mobileBERT achieves the highest accuracy for the "Conjunction" task. This highlights the potential of mobileBERT as a viable option for NLP question answering tasks and reinforces its suitability for various applications in natural language processing.

De-Identification of Protected Health Information PHI from Free Text in Medical Records (2018)

Publication, Philips, India

This work focuses on the de-identification of patient notes in electronic health records to protect patient privacy. Manual de-identification is laborious and error-prone, making an automated system necessary. The authors analyze state-of-the-art techniques and select NeuroNER, an AI tool developed by MIT, for de-identifying Indian radiology reports. They simulate 3300 medical records by extracting data from the MIMIC-III dataset and incorporate Indian names, locations, hospital names, and unit names from scraped websites. However, the initial testing reveals unsatisfactory de-identification of certain Indian data. To improve performance, a new pre-trained model is added to handle Indian medical reports, and medical dictionary lookup is used to reduce misclassifications. By combining the results from multiple pre-trained models, including the Indian-specific model, we successfully generate a PHI token list for anonymizing medical records. The approach improves the efficiency and reliability of the NeuroNER system for Indian data, using a training set of 2000 simulated reports and test/validation sets of 1000 and 300 reports, respectively.

The Performance Enhancement of Statistically Significant Bicluster Using Analysis of Variance (2017)

Publication, Springer

This article focuses on enhancing the performance of statistically significant biclusters through the analysis of variance (ANOVA). The study explores different statistical methods for analyzing gene expression levels and concludes that ANOVA is an efficient method for aggregating gene pairs. ANOVA calculates values by comparing the mean value of each group and uses hypothesis testing to calculate p-values. The article also discusses various tests conducted to improve the performance of gene pairs and applies clustering techniques to explore gene expression information for both homogeneous and heterogeneous data. Statistical approaches are used to identify relevant information from gene subsets, and various testing methods are employed to enhance the performance of correlated genes. Comparisons with other biclustering methods, such as paired t-tests and two-sample tests, show that ANOVA's one-sample test produces better results.
